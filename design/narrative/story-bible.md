# Lantern Drift II: The Borrowed Dawn — Story Bible

> Sequel to *Lantern Drift: What the Dark Remembers*. A 2D story platformer in five chapters.
> Status: Approved · This document comes before any gameplay code.

## 1. Theme

**You don't have to glow to matter.** And under it: light that is only ever shared, never renewed, runs out.

The first game asked what we owe the people who saved us. This one asks what happens a generation later, when the gift they gave is nearly spent.

## 2. World background and history

**Hollowmere** is a cave lit by twenty lanterns, home to the Lumen, a firefly people. In the first game the Long Cold nearly ended them; a hatchling named Wick shared his light back and woke them (the "Chorus" ending). That was many years ago.

What the Lumen do not know:

- Their cave sits under **Greywater Rock**, a sea stack with a ruined lighthouse on top.
- The lighthouse keeper, **Maren**, did not burn oil. She raised *lumen-flies* inside the great lens. Thousands of them, glowing together, were the lamp. Every dusk she sang them awake: *"Little light, little light…"*
- Lumen light is **stored sunlight**. Each dawn the flies basked in the lens and were filled for the night.
- One night a storm (the Lumen call it **the Long Night**) and a great dusk-moth drawn to the lamp shattered the lens. The survivors fled down the lighthouse well into the rock, built the Heartlamp as a small copy of the lens they had lost, and kept singing the keeper's lullaby without remembering where it came from.
- Underground, light can be shared but never made. Each generation has been a little dimmer than the last.

## 3. Characters

| Character | Who they are | What they want | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nib** (player) | A young Lumen born with no glow at all. Carries a lantern on a crook because she has none of her own. | To stop being the one everybody is gentle with. To earn a light. | Dry, quick, hides hurt behind jokes. |
| **Wick** | Hero of the first game, now old. Keeper of the Heartlamp. | To reach "the morning" from the lullaby before the light runs out. | Tired, kind, stubborn. Short sentences. |
| **Fiddle** | A blind cave cricket in the Root Cellar. Navigates by sound. | Quiet, and one song he half remembers. | Grumpy, musical, secretly lonely. |
| **Brine** | The last working lamp-warden, a clockwork keeper's helper. Has waited 9,000 nights for Maren to come back. | To finish its duty: see the lamp lit. | Logbook formal. Counts everything. |
| **Sorrel** | Elder from the first game. Nib's teacher. | For Nib to stop apologising for herself. | Warm, plain. |
| **Umbra** ("the Gloam") | The great dusk-moth. Not evil. It has been starving for light since the lamp went out. | Light. Any light. | No words. |
| **Maren** | The keeper. Long gone. Present only in her journal pages. | She loved the flies. | Practical, tender. |

## 4. Main storyline

On the night of the Festival of the First Lantern, every child lights a lantern from their own glow. Nib cannot. While everyone is looking away from her out of kindness, the **Heartlamp goes dark**: someone has taken the flame and is carrying it up the old Well. One by one the Lumen grey and fall asleep. Only Nib stays awake, because she has no light to lose.

She takes the last ember in a lantern and climbs after the thief.

The climb is a climb through her people's forgotten history: the Root Cellar where the first refugees counted their generations in fading scratches; the Drowned Works where humans raised Lumen in jars; the Lighthouse where a clockwork warden still polishes a dead lamp.

The thief is **Wick**. He is not stealing the light. He is trying to save it: the light is running out, and Nib, born dark, is simply the first child for whom nothing was left. He is carrying the last flame to the morning the lullaby promised, to ask for more. He cannot finish the climb. She can.

At the top there is no kind sun waiting, only storm, a broken lens, and Umbra, who comes for any flame. Nib, who has spent her life being the dark one, is the only one who can work unseen. She turns the keeper's three sun-mirrors east. Then the real morning comes.

## 5. Chapter-by-chapter

Every chapter adds one mechanic and one piece of truth.

### I · Hollowmere, Unlit
- **Beat:** The festival. Nib can't light her lantern. The Heartlamp is taken; everyone sleeps except her.
- **Truth added:** Being dark is why she is still awake.
- **Mechanic:** Move, jump, climb. Take the lantern. Light wayside lamps (checkpoints: she is a lamplighter).
- **Environment:** Twenty festival lanterns, visibly dimmer for the younger families. Children's chalk drawings of "morning" as a giant lantern in the sky.

### II · The Root Cellar
- **Beat:** Meets Fiddle. Glimpses the thief. It is Wick.
- **Truth added:** Her darkness is useful. Things up here hunt light.
- **Mechanic:** **Hooding the lantern.** Lit: you can see, glowcaps bloom into bridges, hidden chalk shows. Hooded: Snuffs ignore you.
- **Enemy:** *Snuff* — roosts overhead, dives at a lit lantern and puts it out.
- **Puzzle:** The Root Door opens to three song-bulbs rung in the right order. The order is in the old chalk, which only shows in lantern light… right under a Snuff roost.
- **Decision:** Fiddle's song is missing its middle. Nib finds the keeper's music cylinder. Give it to him, or keep it as proof for home.
- **Environment:** Generation tally marks, each row scratched fainter than the last.

### III · The Drowned Works
- **Beat:** Human-sized machines. Jars. The keeper's flooded desk. Meets Brine.
- **Truth added:** The Lumen were the lighthouse lamp.
- **Mechanic:** Lure lamps. Big human lamps draw wardens and mirelings away.
- **Enemies:** *Mireling* — follows light along under the water and leaps at it. *Warden* — clockwork patrol; sees a lit lantern from far away, a hooded one only up close; leaves its post to snuff any lamp left burning.
- **Puzzle:** Three drain valves. Their settings are chalked on a mural a warden patrols past.
- **Decision (set up):** Brine asks when the keeper is coming back.

### IV · The Lighthouse Stair
- **Beat:** Catches Wick. He has collapsed on the stair.
- **Truth added:** The light is not being stolen. It is running out. Nib was born dark because nothing was left to inherit.
- **Mechanic:** Mirrors. Turn them to carry a beam to a door.
- **Puzzle:** One mirror, then three with a false path.
- **Decision:** Tell Brine the truth about Maren, or let it keep waiting.
- **Environment:** 9,000 tally marks. Moth dust. Lens shards. A child's drawing: a lighthouse full of fireflies.

### V · The Lamp Room
- **Beat:** Storm. The broken lens. Umbra. Then sunrise.
- **Truth added:** The morning is real. Lumen light is sunlight. Nobody was broken.
- **Challenge:** Turn three sun-mirrors east before dawn. Umbra hunts whatever is lit, so Nib works hooded and uses the braziers to draw it away: everything the climb taught her.
- **Payoff:** Dawn through the mirrors. Umbra flies into the light and is finally full. Nib glows for the first time. Wick sees the morning.

## 6. Plot twists (in order)

1. The thief is the hero of the first game.
2. The Lumen were never cave people. They were the lighthouse lamp.
3. Nothing is wrong with Nib. The light ran out, and she is where it ran out.
4. The monster is hungry for the same thing they are. And the lullaby's morning was never a metaphor.

## 7. Puzzle integration

| Puzzle | What it teaches | Why it belongs to the story |
|---|---|---|
| Glowcap bridges | Light makes paths; light draws danger | Nib must choose when to be seen |
| Song-bulb door | Read the hidden chalk | The refugees left instructions for their children |
| Drain valves | Observe under pressure | The keeper's working notes, still on the wall |
| Lure lamps | Use light as a tool, not just a need | Wardens only ever wanted to tend lamps |
| Mirrors | Carry light where you are not | Rehearsal for carrying the dawn |
| Umbra | All of the above, at once | Her whole "flaw" is the solution |

## 8. Key dialogue

**Opening (Sorrel, festival):** "Hold it up, Nib. It doesn't matter if it lights."
**Nib:** "It matters a little."

**Fiddle, on meeting:** "You're loud for something so dark. Shut that lantern. The Snuffs have ears in their eyes."

**Brine:** "Night nine thousand and four. Lamp: unlit. Keeper: expected."

**Wick, on the stair:** "I wasn't stealing it, little one. I was running. There isn't any more where this came from."
**Nib:** "Then why was I born with none?"
**Wick:** "Because we'd spent it. You're not broken, Nib. You're just last."

**Wick, at dawn:** "So that's what they were singing about."

## 9. Endings

The final choice, made in full daylight:

- **Carry the Morning Down** — Nib leaves the mirrors turned. Every dawn a beam falls down the Well into the Heartlamp. Hollowmere stays home, and is never dark again.
- **Call Them Up** — Nib rings the keeper's bell down the Well. The Lumen climb into the sky they came from. Hollowmere is left as a memory.

Shaped by earlier decisions:

- **Fiddle** — If he was given the cylinder, he plays the whole lullaby in the Lamp Room (Umbra lingers longer at the braziers), and is there at the end.
- **Brine** — Told the truth: "Duty complete." It powers down in the sun, at rest. Left waiting: it goes on polishing the lamp, and Nib visits.
- **Wick** — Always sees the morning. His light goes out gently in it.

Last line, either way: **Nib lights the first lantern of the day from her own glow.**
